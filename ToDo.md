# vega-webgpu roadmap

Tracking the gap to a production-grade, canvas-matching WebGPU renderer.

Open work comes first, grouped by what it blocks. Everything already landed is kept below, grouped by theme, because most of it records a decision and the measurement behind it rather than just a change.

The goal for 2.0.0 is narrow: every mark and mark property vega can express is drawn, the output matches the canvas renderer, and no spec is slower than it. Anything that would be an improvement on canvas rather than parity with it waits.

## How a difference is measured

`WEBGPU_FIDELITY=1 npx playwright test fidelity` ranks every spec and fixture by three numbers, which say different things. `diff` is the share of pixels that moved, `max` is how wrong the worst block of two scene pixels is, and `mean` is the average error over inked pixels. A soft antialiasing difference is a high diff and a low max, a misplaced mark is the reverse, and a systematic one shows in the mean. The per-spec gate in `render.spec.ts` uses `diff` plus a densest-32px-tile measure, since a whole-image percentage is diluted by however much of a chart is empty.

Currently 184 cases, 35 of them matching canvas to within 2 levels everywhere.

```
by max channel error   0-2: 37   2-8: 17   8-32: 87   32-128: 43   over 128: 2
by mean channel error  0-1: 102   1-2: 28   2-4: 31   4-8: 18    over 8: 7
```

Read together those two say where the work is. Systematically we are close, 130 of 186 averaging under two levels over their inked pixels. Only two are over 128 and both are skipped with a reason, `clip-path-round` at 179 and `gradient-diagonal` at 177. `text-variants` was a third at 180.5 and reads 1.0 now that a stroked glyph is given room for its miter, and `line-curve-caps` a fourth at 212 and reads 27.8 now that a curve draws its round cap.

What is left is local, and it is not one cause. Past the two skips the worst are `label` at 113, which is vega translating to each item and back and is an upstream report below, `contour-map` at 99, `symbol-shapes` at 74 and `playfair` at 71, then the map and choropleth family at 60 to 69, which is the polygon seam model. Each is a few pixels in one place: `contour-map` differs on 0.023% of its frame and its interiors read 0.94 over 174k flat pixels.

## Skipped in the render suite

Two fixtures are recorded and shown but not gated. A skip is the alternative to raising a budget past what a measure reports, which would write a defect down as if it were a tolerance. Each carries the release it is queued for in `skippedScenes` in [test/render/scenes.ts](test/render/scenes.ts), and that milestone reaches the gallery as a badge on the row and a banner over the case, and the test report as an annotation. The pngs are recorded either way, so the difference each one names is on screen beside the cases that pass.

| fixture | queued for | what is wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `clip-path-erase` | 2.1.0 | an erasing compositing operator inside a clip path erases the box of the path rather than the path |
| `gradient-diagonal` | upstream | canvas draws a diagonal ramp as a pattern at the group origin and leaves marks blank. We are the correct side of this one |

`gradient-diagonal` is not ours: matching canvas there would mean deliberately not drawing marks, so it wants the upstream report below rather than a fix here.

Eight more specs skip on a run, and none of them is a defect. They are the diagnostics that only run when asked: six under `WEBGPU_BENCH`, one under `WEBGPU_FIDELITY` and one under `WEBGPU_DIAGNOSE`. A ninth is the half pixel baseline case in `textdrift.spec.ts`, which has nothing to reproduce at an even pixel ratio and so skips at dpr 2 only. A full run reports 10 skipped at dpr 1 and 11 at dpr 2, and two of those are the table above.

## Open: needed for 2.0.0

### Feature completeness

Each of these is a vega property that is parsed and then ignored, so a spec using it renders differently with nothing said.

- [x] **A round `strokeCap` on a curved line is drawn.** The curve shaders cover the stroke across its width and stop at the end points, so a basis, cardinal, monotone, catmull-rom or natural line ended flat. Routing a capped curve to the tessellated path caps it and moves the curve itself further from canvas, measured as 80 differing pixels going to 145, so the cap is drawn where the curve is instead: each end of each run gets a two point stub along the end tangent, through the segment shader, with a butt at the end point and a round cap at the far end. The butt is what keeps it to the half beyond the curve, since a whole disc would sit over the curve body as well and composite twice there under any mode that reads the frame back. The writers report their own runs rather than the stubs being read back out of the instance rows: a basis span carries four B-spline control points and starts at (P0 + 4*P1 + P2) / 6 rather than at P0, so the rows cannot say where a run ends, and reading them that way put a disc at every span boundary. A cubic can also repeat its endpoint as a control point, so the tangent falls back through the other two rather than leaving that end uncapped. `line-curve-caps` goes from 0.279% of pixels and a worst block of 212 to 0.020% and 27.8, inside every budget, and is gated rather than skipped.
- [x] **`strokeCap` square no longer drops `strokeDash`.** `strokeAsSegments` sent a square capped mark to the extruded ribbon, on the grounds that only extrude-polyline draws a square cap. That stopped being true when `writeSegments` learned to grow a run by half a stroke at each outer end, which is the same shape, and rule, shape and line have relied on it since. arc, area, path and trail still took the old branch, and the branch is the only place the dash is applied, so the pattern was computed and thrown away. The gate is gone, so all four walk their outline whatever the cap, and two more call sites that read `caps` and `join` off `strokeEnds` while leaving `square` behind are fixed with it: shape dropped the cap on a solid stroke and kept it on a gradient one, and the line mark's own segment writer dropped it, which is why `lineRoute` forced a square capped line onto the tessellated path. It forces only a curve there now, since the curve shaders draw no cap at all. The new `square-dashes` fixture is the four marks dashed under a square cap with a line as the control, and reads worst channel 212 against canvas before the fix and 122 after, all of it one row at a dash end where the phase around a ring differs.
- [x] **A gradient filled symbol blends its stroke.** The fill took `gradientPipelineFor(blendKey(item.blend))` and the stroke a fixed `solidPipeline` built with normal blend, so a symbol with a ramp and a `blend` drew a ring that ignored it. `solidPipelineFor` is the same `blendPipelines` helper the fill uses. The new `blend-symbol-gradient` fixture reads worst channel 194 with the stroke pinned to normal and 90 with it routed, the residual being a three pixel band inside the stroke of a tessellated ring.
- [x] **A gradient stroke reaches every mark that strokes, and `rule` draws its stroke caps.** symbol, rect, group, line and rule stroke through a shader of their own, a distance function or an analytic box, and neither can express a ramp, so each takes the outline walk a dash already takes. `rule` had no cap either: a round one is the segment shader's own end, and a square one is a butt end on a segment half a stroke longer at each end, which is the same shape. Measured on the playground against canvas, with the property on and against the mark plain: symbol 0.012% of pixels to 0.065%, rect 0.000% to 0.000%, group 0.000% to 0.000%, line 0.046% to 0.090%, rule 0.002% to 0.004%, and rule's cap 0.014%. Two things had to be right in the group: a ramp is per item so its border cannot join the batched one, and the border is held back until after the backgrounds, which is the order canvas paints them in. Enqueuing it early left the group's own fill over the inner half of its own border, four pixels of a four pixel stroke. The support table the playground drives has no `no` left in it, and `gradient-strokes` grew a symbol, rect, rule and line to hold it.
- [x] **A round join drew one disc and butted the next segment into it, which doubles under a blend.** The join encoding had a free slot, a bisector with no cut length, and a round join is exactly that: each segment keeps its own side of the bisector and rounds off at the end point, so the two halves of the disc are drawn once each. Before, the arriving segment drew the whole disc and the leaving one butted into it, which overlaps its body for half a stroke. Invisible under an opaque stroke and not under `multiply`, which multiplies the overlap twice. On the playground a line with `strokeCap` and `blend` together went from 0.413% of pixels to 0.107%, against 0.104% for the cap alone and 0.093% for the blend alone, and the new `line-join-blend` fixture reads 0.000% at both pixel ratios where it read 0.117% before. `buttRound` is gone with it.
- [x] **A cached symbol buffer was being made in the frame pool, so every frame after the third submitted a destroyed one.** `[Buffer "Symbol Geometry Buffer"] used in submit while destroyed`, on any scene holding a symbol shape with no distance function, which is `cross`, `stroke` and a custom path. The pool destroys a buffer two frames after the one that made it, and the shape cache went on handing out the same one. Nothing in the suite saw it, because WebGPU reports a misuse through `onuncapturederror` and carries on drawing, and nothing was listening: the pixels were right. `gpuerrors.spec.ts` listens now and redraws seven fixtures six times each, which reproduces it at seven errors without the fix and none with it.
- [x] **A line mark held a bind group across a change of blend, which loses the whole frame.** The segment draw cached its uniform bind group on the buffer alone, so a scene with one blended line and one unblended one set a group built from the normal pipeline's layout on the blended pipeline. That invalidates the command buffer, and with it every mark of the frame: `dash-caps` came out entirely blank, background included. Nothing said so, because the message goes to `onuncapturederror`. The cache is keyed by pipeline as well now. `blendKey` also warned that `normal` needs a destination read, since only `source-over` and nothing were treated as the default and vega writes both.
- [x] **Two facing round caps across a dash gap are cut at the midpoint rather than overlapped.** A round cap reaches half the stroke past the end of its run, so on any dash whose gap is under the stroke width the two runs either side overlap. Canvas fills the union of the bands once and we drew a band each, which composites the overlap twice: nothing under an opaque stroke, a dark bar at every gap under a blend. With equal radii each arc is the outer one on its own side, so cutting both at the midpoint traces the union exactly. The encoding had a free slot for it, a round end with a positive cut, and the cut is hard rather than a ramp for the same reason the bisector is: two ramps facing each other across one plane both report a half and compose to three quarters, which is a pale seam at every joint. On the playground a line with `strokeDash`, `strokeCap` and `blend` went from 0.779% of pixels to 0.262%, all five properties at once from 0.735% to 0.202%, and a rule from 0.275% to 0.044%. Merging the runs instead is exact only for a square cap, where the two caps fill the gap between them, and `dashPolyline` does that: two round caps cross short of the stroke edge and leave a notch either side that a merged run paints over, which measured worse than the overlap. The cut follows the line between the two ends rather than either direction, so it holds around a corner as readily as along a straight run, which is where a dash gap most often lands: at `strokeDashOffset` 22 on a big corner the two caps met across the turn and left a dark lens in the middle of it. It applies only past the end point, since carried into the band it slices a wedge off the side canvas draws, with a hard edge where canvas has an antialiased one. It shares the miter's encoding and is told apart by its length, which is always under half the stroke where a miter's is never under it. Two unit mistakes on the way, both caught at dpr 2 and invisible at dpr 1: the vertex stage measures in item units and the fragment in device pixels, since the vertex stage scales the cut across with everything else, and scaling it again in the fragment cut away most of the stroke. The new `dash-corner` fixture is one big corner dashed four ways and reads 0.018% of pixels against 0.531% with the cut off. On the playground a line with `strokeDash`, `strokeCap` and `blend` is 0.204% where it was 0.913%, all five properties at once 0.144%, and the blend now adds almost nothing over the dash alone at 0.135%. Past the end point the plane is the whole answer, since out there both shapes are only their caps. Inside the band the test is instead whether the cap it faces covers the pixel outright: around a sharp corner that cap reaches across the turn into this run's body, and cutting the band on the plane instead would slice a wedge off the side canvas draws. Without either, both drew it, which reads as a dark bisector through every corner. On the playground a dashed, capped, blended line goes from 9 pixels over 60 levels out to 1, its 0.913% of pixels to 0.173%, and `dash-corner` from 0.531% with the cut off to 0.008%.
- [x] **A square cap is drawn on a dash.** `capEnd` gave a square cap a butt end, so every dash run of a square-capped stroke was half a stroke short at each end. The writer lengthens the run instead, which is the same shape, and `rule` stops doing its own version of it. The `dash-caps` fixture holds a round, a square and a butt dash, blended and not, with a gap under the stroke width and one over it.
- [x] **`strokeDash` reaches every mark that strokes.** line, rule, group, rect, symbol, path, area, shape, arc and trail all walk their outline and dash it through the segment shader, since a dash cannot come out of an extruded ribbon or a shader that draws a whole ring. `strokeOutline` and `dashPatternOf` in marks/util.ts are the one implementation. Two things were wrong and neither was the phase the earlier note claimed. The dash was enqueued before the fill, so an opaque fill covered the inner half of every run: on `path-dashes` that was 400 of the 416 differing pixels, and fixing the order alone took it from 0.887% to 0.009% and mean 4.32 to 0.15. What was left was the seam of a closed contour, where the run that reaches the start point and the one that leaves it met as two flat ends and left the corner unpainted at worst channel 182. `dashPolyline` now joins those two runs when the input is a loop, so the seam is a vertex like any other. `path-dashes` holds 0.000% of pixels and worst channel 45 at the default budgets. The playground measures every mark against canvas with a dash on: group 0.000%, rect 0.083%, area 0.113%, shape 0.119%, line 0.135%, symbol 0.185%, path 0.193%, trail 0.530%, arc 0.734%, and rule 0.011%.
- [x] **A dashed group border follows the corner radius.** `dashedBorderInstances` walked the box as four straight sides, so a group with both `cornerRadius` and `strokeDash` got a dashed sharp rectangle. The outline now comes from vega's own `pathRectangle` generator, so the curve, where it starts and which way it runs are the ones canvas dashes along. The new `group-corner-dash` fixture holds 0.010% of pixels, mean 0.44 and worst channel 87. The residual is not the flattening: the contour is half a pixel shorter than the curve around the whole border, and cutting the corners finer to close that moved the dashes further from canvas rather than nearer (0.010% to 0.065%, 87 to 175), which says canvas is not measuring the curve either. Measured again over four cases at three settings, with the arc conversion fixed underneath it, and the coarse contour wins every one of them: at `DASH_FLATNESS` 1, 2 and 4, `gradient-strokes` reads 0.031%, 0.034% and 0.047% of pixels, `group-corner-dash` 0.010%, 0.031% and 0.033%, `mark-dashes` 0.032%, 0.038% and 0.043%, and the playground dashed arc 0.535%, 0.623% and 0.636%. So a dash is walked along a contour cut at 1 whatever the pixel ratio, and the fill is drawn on one cut at four times the ratio.
- [x] **rect and arc draw `strokeDash` too, so every mark that strokes does.** A rect stroke is analytic in the fragment shader and an arc is a triangulated ring, so neither can express a pattern: both take the outline walk the rest use, rect through `dashedBorderInstances` and arc through `dashedOutline` on its own contours. Measured on the playground against canvas, rect goes 2.616% of pixels to 0.083% and arc 2.739% to 0.734%, the latter under its own 0.816% with no dash at all. A dashed rect edge is pixel for pixel canvas's, and the three wedges of `mark-dashes` differ by 232, 248 and 226 pixels with the solid one in the middle, so what is left on an arc is its ribbon edge and not the dash.
- [x] **A stroked trail is the widest of the triangulated marks, and the cause is compositing rather than geometry.** vega writes one closed contour per trail segment and consecutive contours overlap, so two antialiased stroke fringes land on the same pixel and we composited both where canvas fills the union of the bands once. At the worst pixel canvas read 209, 111, 111 and we read 131, 79, 81, which is more stroke and not less. Not the flattening, which the first note assumed: at `CURVE_FLATNESS` 4, 8 and 16 the fixture read 0.174%, 0.184% and 0.181% of pixels, and dropping the simplification took the plain `trail` fixture to 5.984% since tess2 refuses the unsimplified contours.

  The stroke is drawn as one shape now. The bands go into a single sampled `r8unorm` mask under a max blend, where the largest coverage a pixel receives is the union of them, and a quad over the box the instances reach paints the colour through it in one composite with the item's own blend. The frame's render pass breaks either side of the mask pass and resumes with a load, so the MSAA resolve stays on the last of them. A gradient stroke still goes band by band, since a ramp cannot be read back out of a coverage mask.

  The new `trail-overlap` fixture is a wide ribbon stroked translucently and again under multiply, and it reads 0.046% of pixels where it read 3.645%, with the worst tile 0.6% against 25.8% and the signed mean 0.27 against 3.54. `trail-stroked` goes 0.172% to 0.048%, and 0.049% to 0.019% at dpr 2. What is left is where two contour strokes cross at an acute angle: the mask keeps the larger of the two coverages and the union is larger still, so the tip of each crossing is one pixel light. Summing them instead, or screening them, puts the double composite back (3.580% against 3.645% for no mask at all), because the bands mostly overlap along their length rather than crossing. One measurement went on the pass split: a render pass carrying a `timestampWrites` with neither index set is rejected, which invalidated the whole command buffer and drew a black frame. `gpuerrors.spec.ts` named it in one run.

- [x] **A blend over a translucent source is inexact.** The new `blend-alpha` fixture puts all four modes at four source alphas over a two-tone opaque backdrop, and it says which: multiply and screen are exact at every alpha (worst channel 1), since their factors expand to the formula canvas uses. min and max have no term to interpolate with, so darken and lighten read 44 and 38 levels out at half alpha. The first note blamed multiply for 3.0% of pixels, which the fixture does not support.

  Those two are evaluated in a shader instead. The mark draws unblended into a layer of its own, the frame is copied out beneath it, and a composite evaluates `as*(1-ab)*Cs + as*ab*B(Cb,Cs) + (1-as)*ab*Cb` over the two and replaces the pixel. `blend-alpha` goes from a mean of 4.55 and a worst channel of 44 to 0.08 and 2.

- [x] **Text gradients shared a cache slot.** The glyph raster bakes the fill, because it is drawn through vega's own canvas text mark, and `textCacheKey` joined `item.fill` straight in. Every object stringifies the same way, so one string drawn with three different gradients shared a raster and the last two wore the first one's colours. A paint that is an object is now serialized. The `text-gradients` fixture is three identical labels differing only in their gradient, and it measures 6.671% of pixels and mean 72.59 without the fix against 0.000% and mean 0.34 with it.
- [x] **A blend mode needs an opaque backdrop.** The same formula carries `as*(1-ab)*Cs`, the part of the source landing where the backdrop does not reach, and factors that reach the destination cannot carry that as well: a multiply over nothing came out black where canvas draws the source plainly. The layer above answers it, and when to pay for it is exact rather than guessed: source over leaves the alpha at one, so a frame cleared opaque is opaque wherever anything draws and multiply and screen can stay on the blend state there. A frame with no background takes the layer for every mode.

  What it costs, measured on the real adapter at 1600x1200 with a full height bar per draw: a frame of 1, 8 and 32 of them takes 9.03, 9.53 and 11.15 ms against 8.60, 8.95 and 9.25 for the same marks under multiply, so a layered draw is about 0.06 ms. The suite's own software adapter says 21 ms for the same draw, which is what rasterizing three full frame passes on the cpu costs and not what anything else sees. Worth writing down: the first reading said 717 ms for 32 of them and was entirely an artefact of the runner.

  The routing is central. `markPipeline` is the only place a mark pipeline is built, so a mode that needs the backdrop is built unblended there and the mode it was asked for is recorded against the pipeline. The queue reads that back and lifts the draw into a layer run, folding it in straight after, which also means each draw meets the frame on its own the way canvas composites a fill and then a stroke. No mark needed changing for it.

  The new `blend-transparent` fixture is all four modes over a half transparent backdrop and over none at all, on rect, symbol, line and arc, and and with the layer disabled it reads 15.791% of pixels, a mean of 32.70 and a worst channel of 159 against 0.000%, 0.10 and 40 with it. Three bugs came out of writing it. d3 gives a fully transparent colour NaN channels whatever was written, and those reached the clear value as they were, so a spec with a transparent background failed the frame outright. The symbol mark held one uniform bind group across a change of pipeline, so a mark whose items carry different blends set a bind group from the wrong layout and lost the frame. And a symbol shape with no distance function drew through a pipeline carrying no blend at all, so `cross`, `wedge` and a custom path never blended.

- [x] **`blend` reaches every mark.** group, image and text built their pipeline without one, so they always composited normal while the other nine honoured it. Each now takes a `pipelineFor(blend)` the way rect and area do. The catch was that a pipeline built with a default layout owns its own bind group layout, so a bind group made from the normal pipeline is invalid on a blend variant and the frame is dropped whole: every group and its backdrop vanished, with the reason only in a device warning. Every bind group now comes from the pipeline its draw uses, and the image texture cache is keyed by blend as well as by sampler. `blend-marks-more` covers group and text against canvas at 0.000%; image follows the same shape but has no fixture of its own.
- [x] **strokeJoin and strokeMiterLimit are honoured, and the default is a miter.** They were not even declared on `StrokeStyle`, so every interior joint of a segment line was rounded where canvas miters, and a sharp peak came out blunt and short. Both segments at a vertex now carry the same outward bisector and each keeps its own side of it, so their union is the joined outline: the miter reaches its apex on its own, since the bisector leaves the stroke exactly there, and a corner past the limit is cut back to the line canvas bevels along, which is perpendicular to the bisector at `halfWidth * cos`. One bisector rather than a join primitive per vertex, so there is no overlap between the two segments to composite twice. src/util/join.ts computes it, `writeSegments` carries each join to both sides of its vertex, and `geometryForItem` passes the same two properties to extrude-polyline for the triangulated marks. The new `stroke-joins` fixture walks narrowing apex angles as miter, round, bevel and a limit of 2, and holds 0.000% of pixels at the default budgets. Two details cost a measurement each. A round join is one disc, drawn by the segment arriving at the vertex, and letting both draw it composited the same disc twice and darkened its rim (`line-shapes` 67 to 78). And a pixel whose centre lands exactly on the bisector, which every axis aligned corner has, was painted by both, so the tie goes to one of them by the sign each segment already disagrees on (`group-stroke` 51 to 102 without it).
- [x] **A curve is flattened four times as finely, which every consumer wants but a dash.** `svg-path-contours` was run at its default, so a contour sat visibly inside its own curve, and once the strokes stopped being extruded ribbons that became the leading term rather than a detail under the MSAA quantization. The fixtures: `arc-shapes` 0.211% of pixels to 0.058% and its worst tile 2.9% to 0.7%, `gradient-strokes` 0.161% to 0.054%, `path-shapes` 0.034% to 0.005%, `area-shapes` max 74 to 57, `trail` max 81 to 61. The specs follow: `horizon` to 0.000% and max 59 to 45, `area` max 127 to 93, `choropleth-stroked` 153 to 145, `map-bind` 169 to 163, and the mean drops on every map. At dpr 2 it is larger still, and three fixtures left the dpr budget table: gradient-strokes, arc-shapes and trail hold their own budget there now. Two things bound it. A dash is measured along the polyline rather than drawn on it, so canvas is not walking the curve either and a closer contour walks the dashes further off: those take `DASH_FLATNESS` and stay coarse, worth 0.037% against 0.055% on `mark-dashes` and 0.010% against 0.034% on a dashed rounded border. The flatness itself follows the device grid, which took the corner fix below before it was safe.
- [x] **A gradient stroke can be dashed.** Only the extruded ribbon could sample a ramp, and a ribbon carries no dash, so a stroke that asked for both drew solid and the dash was dropped without a word. The segment shader has a gradient entry now, reading the ramp from the fragment world position the way the fill shader does, and every mark that outlines routes a gradient stroke through it: arc, area, trail, path and shape. Measured on the playground with both on, against each mark with neither: arc 0.424% of pixels to 0.451%, area 0.029% to 0.117%, path 0.110% to 0.112%, shape 0.056% to 0.126%, trail 0.167% to 0.452%, so the combination is at or under what the mark costs plain. shape keeps its held outline buffer for a solid stroke and only a gradient one pays for a draw of its own, since a ramp is per item and cannot share a bind group. `gradient-strokes` grew a dashed gradient arc and path to hold it.
- [x] **A round stroke cap reaches path, area, shape, arc and trail.** It rode in with the item above: those marks stroke through the segment shader now, and a `JoinEnd` in src/util/join.ts already carried the cap. extrude-polyline has no round cap at all and drew butt silently. Measured with one on, path 0.121% of pixels, area 0.122% and shape 0.126%, each inside its own budget. What is left is narrower than the item was: a line whose curve the GPU evaluates goes through the curve shader, which has no cap of any kind, and a rule draws its own rect, so both still butt.
- [x] **A group background paints over an earlier sibling's children, the way vega draws it.** Every background went into one instanced draw before any group was visited, so a later group's fill landed under what an earlier one had already drawn outside its own box. The backgrounds interleave with the visit when any group's box reaches into another's bounds, and keep the single draw when none does, which is what a row of panels looks like. The bounds rather than the box is the point: a panel's axis labels reach outside it and the next panel's fill covers them. The new `group-overlap` fixture is a pair that overlaps and a pair that does not, and holds 0.000% of pixels and worst channel 1. Forced back onto the batched path it measures 0.889% of pixels, a 46.9% worst tile and worst channel 196, so the fixture is testing the thing. Nothing in the corpus takes the interleaved path, which is the same as saying no spec here overlaps its panels, so the batch is intact everywhere it was.
- [x] **Routing lines through extrude-polyline for miter parity was the wrong fix.** It was tried and measured worse, not better: `blend-marks` went 0.024% to 0.912% and worst channel 141 to 161, because that path overlaps at a joint too and takes its edge coverage from MSAA. The bisector cut above gets the miter with neither cost.

### Matching canvas

- [x] **Stroked text is drawn whole.** A label is rasterized into an atlas cell sized from vega's own text bound, and that bound is the fill glyph box: `textMetrics` reports an advance and a height and neither knows about a stroke. The cell gave one pixel of clearance for antialiasing, so a stroke was cut off at every edge. Half the width is not the reach either, which is what the first fix assumed and why it left a worst block of 87.8: a glyph corner under a miter join carries out to `miterLimit * width / 2`, the bound canvas itself clamps to and the one `geometryForItem` already pads by. `text-variants` goes from 0.161% of pixels and a worst block of 180.5 to 0.000% and 1.0, and is gated rather than skipped. The other five text fixtures are unmoved.
- [ ] **A ramp that is not axis aligned is a pattern in vega, placed at the group origin.** `util/canvas/gradient.js` builds a canvas gradient only when the ramp is horizontal, vertical or the bounds are square, and renders anything else into an image handed to `createPattern(image, 'no-repeat')`, which is vega #2365. `drawPath` fills after the item translate has been undone, so the pattern lands at the enclosing group's origin rather than at the mark, and a mark further out than its own bounds are wide is filled with nothing at all. The new `gradient-diagonal` fixture is three non-square diagonal rects, of which canvas draws one partly and leaves two blank, with a square box and a horizontal ramp as controls: it reads 23.057% of pixels, a 100% worst tile and a signed mean of 76.52, and is skipped with that measurement. Matching it means deliberately not drawing marks, so it is a decision rather than a fix, and it wants reporting upstream first. A `symbol` reads differently again, drawn everywhere with a ramp that advances with x, and this did not pin down why.

It wants true area coverage, which is the item above, and it is one pixel per cusp. `driving` holds the same thing on a curve.
- [x] **A blended or translucent line no longer composites its own joint twice.** The dark blob on the `blend-marks` chevron was a `line` at `blend: multiply` bending at 165,160: segments were one capsule each, and an interior joint was the round end of one sitting on the butt start of the next, so the overlap was composited twice where canvas strokes the polyline once. The bisector cut leaves the two segments disjoint, so there is no overlap to composite at all. `blend-marks` goes 0.024% to 0.005% of pixels, worst channel 141 to 53 and mean 0.28 to 0.18.
- [x] **The `blend-unsupported` fixture read as a bug rather than a control.** The name said the renderer cannot blend, when it supports several modes, and the picture is 12 unlabelled swatches. It is now `blend-needs-readback`, which is what actually separates the two fixtures, and each of the three blend fixtures names its modes in draw order and says whether it is expected to match. The gallery shows that description above the numbers. The names are not drawn into the picture on purpose: `blend` holds a worst channel budget of 5 and text is where the renderer differs most, so labelling the pixels would cost the fixture the precision it exists for.

### Landed: features

- [x] **The compositing operators are drawn too, not just the blend modes.** vega's `blend` goes straight to `globalCompositeOperation`, which takes the Porter Duff operators in the same property, and those were warned about and drawn normally. What separates them from a blend mode is small once the composite evaluates the formula: a blend keeps source over's coverage weights and changes `B(Cb, Cs)`, an operator keeps B at the source and changes the weights, so the table carries `fa` and `fb` beside `blend`. destination-over, the four in and out pairs, both atops, xor, lighter and copy. Five of them do something to the frame where the source is absent, so those skip the discard and run on every pixel of the clip, which is what canvas does with them. The new `blend-operators` fixture is one clipped group each, since otherwise the erasing ones wipe the ones beside them, and it reads 0.000% of pixels and worst channel 1 where it reads 21.521% and 196 with the table entries taken out. Every value canvas accepts now draws.

- [x] **Line dashes.** `strokeDash`/`strokeDashOffset` are split into drawn runs on the cpu and emitted as segments, covering straight and curved lines. Group and rect borders dash the same way, since the analytic rect stroke cannot express a pattern. Fixture `line-dashes`, 0.000% against canvas.
- [x] **Symbol geometry is already cached** per (shape, size, strokeWidth) with least-recently-used eviction, and instanced: 0.02 to 0.22 ms a frame in the benchmark. Only gradient-filled symbols bypass it.
- [x] **Symbol shapes**: all shapes (square, cross, diamond, triangle-\*, arrow, wedge, stroke, custom SVG) plus `angle` rotation now render. Circles keep the analytic shader. Other shapes are triangulated once per (shape, size) and instanced. `symbol-shapes` 6.8% to 0.03%, `symbol-angle` 14.3% to 0.001%.
- [x] **Gradient on symbols**: gradient-filled symbols (e.g. a legend swatch) now render via the gradient pipeline (triangulated, per item). The `gradient` spec's radial-gradient circle went 4.6% to 0.007%.
- [x] **Trail marks are implemented**, so all twelve of vega's mark types now render. A trail is one filled ribbon whose width follows each point's `size`, sharing the area shader.

  The shared trail path generator took its width from `item.width || item.height`, where vega's own accessor is `item.size || 1`. That drew every trail as a one pixel line rather than a ribbon, and it was only visible because the fixture was compared against canvas. Covered by the `trail` fixture at 0.016%.

- [x] **Blend modes**, for the four WebGPU can express. vega's `blend` is canvas's `globalCompositeOperation`, and WebGPU blends with fixed function state rather than a programmable one, so multiply, screen, darken and lighten fall out of its factors and operations. All four reproduce canvas exactly, worst channel 1, in the `blend` fixture.

  A pipeline is built per blend mode and cached, and a run of rects starts a new draw when the mode changes. The rect shader discards fragments with no coverage: the quad is grown a pixel past the rect, and under a min or max blend those empty fragments would still darken the destination, which is what put a fringe around a `darken` rect.

- [x] **Every mark honours blend**, not just rect. rect, rule, area and symbol reproduce canvas closely (worst channel 1, 1, 1 and 31), and arc, path and line blend correctly and keep the edge error they have without a blend. Covered by the `blend-marks` fixture.

  Every shader now discards a fragment with no coverage. Marks grow their geometry past the shape so an analytic edge is not clipped, and those empty fragments would otherwise still reach the blend state: under a multiply they brighten the destination and under a min they darken it. That alone took rule from 65 to 1.

- [x] **An antialiased edge under a blend weights its own colour now.** Canvas applies a blend as `dst * (1 - a) + a * f(src, dst)`, so the source has to be weighted by its own alpha somewhere, and WebGPU's factors cannot do that and reach the destination at the same time. Every shader is built per blend mode and the fragment entry point applies the weighting, which makes multiply and screen exact at every alpha rather than only at 1. Symbol under multiply went from 88 worst channel to 31, and `blend-marks` from 0.184% to 0.074%.

  min and max have no term to interpolate with, so darken and lighten stay exact only at alpha 0 and 1. Weighting towards the operation's identity (white for min, black for max) at least leaves an antialiased edge alone, where an unweighted colour applies the full blend to a fragment that barely covers the pixel.

### Landed: performance

- [x] **Geometry is cached per item instead of rebuilt every frame.** `cacheShapes` existed but defaulted off and only covered `shape` marks. It is on by default now, bounded to 4096 entries with least-recently-used eviction, and the same helper backs `path` as well. A colour-only change rewrites colours over the cached positions instead of triangulating again. choropleth re-render 67.2 ms to 12.8 ms, which is faster than canvas at 47 ms.
- [x] **Measured on real hardware, and the software rasterizer was most of it.** `WEBGPU_HARDWARE=1` runs the benchmark on the machine's own adapter and drops the forced Vulkan flags, so the browser picks its own backend. Frame cost against SwiftShader: contour-map 40.5 ms to 4.8, choropleth 13.3 to 7.0, airports 8.9 to 4.1, tree-radial-bundle 41.9 to 26.7. choropleth beats canvas by 6.8x (7.0 against 47). Nothing here argues for moving tessellation into a compute shader: on hardware the GPU side is already small, and under SwiftShader it would be adding to the part that is slow.
- [x] **basis and bundle curves are evaluated on the GPU.** tree-radial-bundle is 764 faceted line marks, so every frame built 764 SVG path strings, parsed them, flattened them, extruded a stroke mesh and issued 764 draws. `src/shaders/curve.ts` takes the control points as instances and evaluates the uniform cubic B-spline in the vertex shader, one instance per span, with the joints offset along the analytic tangent so neighbouring quads share an edge exactly. Overlapping them would double-blend, and this spec draws at strokeOpacity 0.2.

  On hardware: line 9.4 ms to 1.1, group 21.8 to 4.8, the spec 27.0 ms to 11.7. The batch only merges draws whose bind groups are the same object, so holding one is what took it from 22.6 to 11.7; rebuilding it per curve silently defeated the merge.

- [x] **Attributed the rest of a frame.** `_draw`, `_submit` and `_reinit` are reported alongside the per-mark timings. tree-radial-bundle spends 3.4 ms in mark drawing and 0.15 ms encoding, so the balance is GPU. It is not vertex bound: 4, 8 and 16 sub-segments per span all cost the same, only fidelity changes. Every spec also carries a ~4 ms floor, which is the benchmark awaiting `onSubmittedWorkDone` and draining the GPU each frame, something real rendering does not do. Treat the totals as upper bounds.
- [x] **Measured what a first frame costs, and prebuilding does not fix it.** First frame against steady state on hardware: tree-radial-bundle 196 ms against 16, choropleth 75 against 7, scatter-plot 40 against 6. That is pipeline setup, and it is worth knowing it is there.

  Creating the pipelines is not the cost. Building every mark's pipelines takes 0.2 ms for all eleven, because the browser defers the real compilation until a pipeline is first used in a draw. Pre-creating them therefore cannot remove it: warming every mark made the first frame 40 to 56 ms _worse_ (symbol-shapes 7.7 to 64.3, scatter-plot 47.8 to 88.4), since it compiles pipelines the spec never draws, and warming only the marks the scene contains measured neutral (scatter-plot 52.3 either way). The warm API was removed rather than shipped as a no-op.

  `createRenderPipelineAsync` is the untried candidate: it is specified to resolve only once a pipeline is usable without further delay, so it should move the compile off the first frame. It needs the render path to await pipelines, which is why it was not tried here.

- [x] **Pipelines are cached by descriptor** in `ctx._pipelineCache`, keyed by shader, entry point, sample count and vertex layout, so marks that share a shader and layout share one pipeline and a `_markCache` reset no longer rebuilds them.
- [x] **Every cubic curve a line draws is evaluated on the GPU.** cardinal, catmull-rom, monotone and natural all come out of d3 as cubic Beziers, so the curve shader gained a `bezier` variant beside its `basis` one and the mark collects the control points from the same generator canvas draws through, with no path string and no flattening in between. `linear` and the `step` family stay on the tessellated path, which is what gives their corners a join.

  Flattening was not just soft, it invented geometry: where `driving`'s cardinal curve doubles back, the flattened polyline turned a smooth reversal into a corner and extrude-polyline mitered it into a spike two pixels above the marker, which canvas does not draw. driving 0.300% to 0.146% and its mean channel error 6.4 to 4.8, line-curves mean 13.1 to 11.7, falkensee 0.102% to 0.080%. Collecting from the generator rather than parsing its path string also made it cheaper than the tessellation it replaced: driving 0.60 ms to 0.58 on the main thread.

- [x] **Uniform buffers are reused across draws.** Every `draw` call minted a uniform buffer and a bind group, and a spec with hundreds of small line marks paid for hundreds of both per frame. They are keyed by resolution and offset now. Note that outright sharing one buffer is wrong: the render queue defers every draw to the end of the frame, so one buffer rewritten per group hands every draw the last group's offset, which is what `line-curves` and `regression` caught.
- [x] **The shader sources actually share their common wgsl now.** Building them in TypeScript is only worth it if the shared parts are shared, and they had been copied: the blend prelude plus fragment entry pair in every file, the fill and stroke pixel share in three, box coverage in two, the zero-length-safe segment direction in three, and the subdivision count in both the shader and the mark, where they had to agree or the curve loses spans. One definition each.

  Area, Path and Shape all named the same builder and Group named rect's, so the same wgsl compiled three and two times and the marks built a pipeline each for layouts that were character identical. On a first frame: choropleth 6 shader modules and 8 pipelines to 5 and 6, arc-diagram 8 and 9 to 7 and 8, bar 5 and 7 to 4 and 5, and choropleth's first frame 135.9 ms to 114.

- [x] **The line mark has one curve draw and one route.** drawBezier was drawBasis with the names swapped, and five predicates read `points[0]` to answer one question, three of them partitioning the interpolate enum separately. `lineRoute` says where a mark draws and `CURVE_OF` is the single table of which cubic each interpolation is. Both curve pipelines had also been built up front, so a scene of linear lines paid to compile shaders it never drew. 133 lines out of line.ts, every spec pixel identical.
- [x] **The line mark batches its segments now, and renderBatch is gone.** The flag was broken three ways: `true` meant one draw per mark and `false` meant accumulate, so the name was inverted; the accumulate path used a second segment shader that never got the analytic coverage `SLine` did; and neither mode batched anything, because the per-mark round join draw flushed the batch every time. Measured: parallel-coords issued 888 draws a frame in both modes.

  The join disc is gone. A segment end can be round, which is both a round stroke cap and the round join at an interior vertex, so the segment shader draws the whole polyline. Only the earlier segment of a joint rounds its end, since rounding both would blend the same disc twice. That leaves one segment path, so the flag has nothing to select between and the duplicate shader is deleted along with the dead `simpleLine`.

  parallel-coords 888 draws a frame to 105, and 3.95 ms to 2.34 on the main thread. Fidelity improved with it: regression 0.023% to 0.006% and its worst channel 73 to 60, stocks-index 0.043% to 0.033%. Diagonal rules hold at 3.1 levels off exact pixel coverage where canvas is 15.9.

- [x] **A NaN coordinate lands at the origin, the way vega reads it.** vega's own accessors are `item.x || 0`, which folds NaN and null to zero. Ours were destructuring defaults and `?? 0`, which only fold undefined, so a NaN reached the vertex buffer and the rasterizer dropped the triangle: the mark vanished where canvas draws it. The earthquakes-globe legend is a real case, since vega gives its symbols NaN x and y and positions each entry by its enclosing group, so webgpu drew no legend symbols at all. The `nan-placement` fixture pins it at 231 worst channel to 1.
- [x] **The render lock releases at submit rather than at the next animation frame.** It coalesced correctly, since only the most recent pending render survives, but `_frame` waited for requestAnimationFrame before releasing, so a render arriving inside that window was deferred a whole frame. The rAF only existed to close a debugLog timing. Deferred render latency, mean and worst: tree-radial-bundle 13.58 and 19.60 ms to 12.47 and 14.30, bar 1.45 and 2.90 to 0.92 and 1.10, scatter-plot 2.28 and 4.00 to 1.74 and 2.10.

  What is left on tree-radial-bundle is its own 12 ms frame cost. `test/render/latency.spec.ts` measures this under WEBGPU_BENCH.

- [x] **Analytic coverage works in device pixels everywhere now.** rect's straight path, rule and sline converted to device pixels, but the analytic circle, the symbol distance fields, the rounded rect edge and the curve stroke all measured coverage in logical pixels. At dpr 1 those are the same number, which is why nothing caught it: the whole suite ran at `deviceScaleFactor: 1`. At dpr 2 the antialiasing came out twice as soft as canvas's.

  `RENDER_DPR` sets the suite's device pixel ratio. At dpr 2 twelve of twenty one fixtures failed before and seven after, and dpr 1 is untouched, all 116 cross-checked cases byte identical.

- [x] **arrow and wedge are drawn analytically.** vega draws a wedge as an isosceles triangle and an arrow as a shaft box under a head triangle, so both fit the existing distance functions. Two bugs came out of it: `sdTriangleInflated` scaled about the centroid, which moves every edge by the same distance only for an equilateral triangle, and the geometry quad assumed a miter reaches about the stroke half width where a wedge tip carries it seven times as far, clipping the tip three pixels short. It scales about the incentre now and each shape substitutes its own reach and miter. symbol-shapes 0.049% to 0.032%.
- [x] **A frame no longer drains the GPU before the next one starts.** `renderAsync` awaited `onSubmittedWorkDone()` and vega awaits `renderAsync` on every frame, so the cpu could never run ahead of the gpu: no overlap, no pipelining. Its own doc comment said it resolved when the frame was submitted, which is what it does now. A caller that wants the pixels reads them back through `captureFrame`, which is ordered behind the frame in the same queue, and `idle()` is there for anyone who genuinely wants to wait. All 116 cross-checked cases byte identical.
- [x] **Frame rate is measured honestly now, on both paths.** The live counter in `test/fps.js` was a bare `requestAnimationFrame` loop that never observed a render, so it reported how often the browser could service rAF: canvas draws synchronously and blocks it, webgpu submits and returns, so at a million symbols it read 2 against 30 without either being frames drawn. It counts renders now, by wrapping the renderer's own entry point, and shows cpu and gpu time beside the rate.

  The gpu number comes from timestamp queries written around the render pass, read back a frame or more later so they never stall the frame being drawn. `renderer.gpuFrameTime` exposes it and the benchmark reports it per spec.

  What it says is worth knowing: gpu time is 0.00 to 0.10 ms against 0.5 to 8 ms of cpu on every spec in the benchmark. The renderer is entirely cpu bound, which is also why replacing the analytic circle's 32-gon with a quad, sixteen times fewer vertices, measured as a wash.

- [x] **The analytic circle draws on a quad rather than a 32-gon.** The fragment shader computes the circle from its own distance, so approximating one in geometry only cost vertices: 96 per symbol against 6, which is 28.8 million a frame at three hundred thousand points. Gpu time at 300k goes 2.62 ms to 0.40.

  Measured at twenty and fifty thousand this looked like a wash, because the gpu was idle there at 0.3 ms. It also reads as a fidelity regression against canvas, eleven specs moving by up to 0.19%, and it is the opposite: scored against exact pixel coverage the quad is closer on every measure, 0.6 levels off against 0.8 for a fill and 2.3 against 2.7 for a stroke, where canvas is 1.5 and 10.4.

- [x] **Symbol colours are written straight into the instance buffer.** `Color.from2` allocates a fresh rgba array on every call and the attribute loops called it twice per item, so a 300k frame minted 600k arrays. renderAsync at 300k goes 13.6 ms to 7.1.
- [x] **Analytic coverage was wrong for anything thinner than a pixel.** `clamp(half - d + 0.5)` is the coverage of a band only when the band is at least a pixel wide. A 0.2 px country border came back as 0.6 covered, three times the ink, which is why geographic outlines looked heavy and broken. Taking the difference of the two edges instead, `clamp(hi - p + 0.5) - clamp(lo - p + 0.5)`, is right at every width. Rules at one to three pixels are unmoved, still 3.1 levels off exact coverage against canvas's 15.9.
- [x] **A shape mark's outline draws as segments rather than a triangulated ribbon.** extrude-polyline built a mesh per contour and MSAA gave its edge quarter step coverage, which a sub pixel border cannot survive. The contours are already flattened, so they go straight to the segment shader. map-point-radius 1.384% to 0.825%, map-bind 0.660% to 0.345%, contour-map 0.058% to 0.008%.

  It is not yet a cpu win. The shape mark still spends 17.5 ms a frame on the globe, and taking the stroke out of the triangulation did not move it, so where that goes wants another look.

- [x] **Shader sources are built in TypeScript, one variant per use.** The `.wgsl` files could not be specialized, so a blend mode meant either branching per pixel or a file per combination. `src/shaders/` is TypeScript now and `shaderModule()` composes a source and compiles it on first use, keyed by name, sub-variant and blend mode. Building on demand rather than up front matters: a chart uses a handful of these, and compiling every blend mode of every shader would put the cost of shaders nobody draws into the first frame. Measured: first frame unchanged, symbol-shapes 117.3 ms against 116.8 and choropleth 133.0 against 135.9, both within the run to run spread. 120 of 121 specs pixel-identical.

  This also removed `rollup-plugin-string`, collapsed the three byte-identical area/path/shape shaders into one builder, and moved the projection and gradient blocks each into a single shared snippet.

- [x] **A gradient stroke samples the ramp.** The earthquakes-globe sphere strokes with a linear gradient and used to come out flat teal against canvas's blue ramp. A gradient stroke keeps the triangulated ribbon and goes through the gradient pipeline, since the segment shader cannot sample per fragment, and shape, arc, area, path and trail all take that route. symbol, rect, rule and line still draw a gradient stroke as a flat colour.

### Landed: robustness and bugs

- [x] **A blended line drawn last lost its blend.** The open batch only joined the queue at submit, after the frame had decided it needed no off-frame pass, so the line drew straight into the frame. `blend-line-last` read 212 on the worst block.
- [x] **Gradient stops at zero alpha faded from black.** d3-color drops the channels of a colour with no alpha. Stops parse through canvas's own `fillStyle` now. `gradient-alpha-stop` read 226.5.
- [x] **Text took its blend into the glyph atlas.** vega's text mark sets the blend on the context it draws into, so `source-atop` and the other operators rasterized nothing. `text-blend-atop` read 152.
- [x] **Every label in a text mark took the first label's blend.** The labels went up in one draw with one pipeline. They close a run on a change of blend now, as the other marks do. `text-blend-mixed` read 160.
- [x] **A group mark with `clip: true` was not clipped, and a clip inside a rounded group kept its corners.** Each mark worked out its own clip, and the group mark had none, while the rest took the group's box without its radius. The renderer applies a mark's clip before it draws it now, the way vega's does, and through the same code as a clipping group item. `group-mark-clip` read 180 and `clip-mark-rounded` 169.
- [x] **Stroked text with a miter limit of 0 was clipped.** The cell read the limit with `??` where canvas treats 0 as 10. `text-miter-zero` read 208.8.
- [x] **The playground and the demo page leaked a GPU device on every change.** `View.finalize` stops at the view. On CI the playground spec stalled creating devices.
- [x] **Smaller ones.** Scaled paths keep their scale on the tessellation fallbacks, the path key carries the flatness so a pixel ratio change re-triangulates fills, the uniform cache is LRU and frees what it evicts, and the zoom listener holds its renderer weakly.

- [x] **A group border held back to after its children lost the whole frame under a blend.** The draw picked its pipeline by mode and then set the bind group built from the plain one, which is the third time that shape of bug has turned up: a bind group belongs to the layout it was made from, and the command buffer is thrown out whole. - [x] **Five outline routes ignored `blend` entirely.** A dashed group border, a held group border, a dashed diagonal rule, a gradient stroked line and a shape's outline all reached their draw through a pipeline fixed at normal, so the mode was read, passed along and then dropped. It was false for the life of the renderer, so every blended mark paid for an MSAA resolve, a copy of the frame and two more render passes. Not one pixel moved, because the slow path is exact as well, which is why the whole corpus passed. It is read per frame now, and the backdrop state is part of the pipeline cache key, since a pipeline built for one backdrop is wrong for the other and the key had only the mode. `blendpath.spec.ts` asserts which of the two paths six fixtures take, and it fails against the old reading.

- [x] **A trail's stroke ignored any blend the state could not express.** The composite that paints a coverage mask into the frame built its pipeline straight from `blendState`, so a trail with `darken` was wrong the same way every mark was before the layer existed, and one with `overlay` drew unblended. It goes through the same `buildBlend` every mark pipeline does now, which lifts it into a layer and folds it in from there.

- [x] **The mask and blend targets were built on every frame whatever drew.** Four textures the size of the canvas, about 48 MB at 1600x1200 with four samples, created for a renderer that never masks or blends. The queue records whether anything drew off the frame and they are built only then.

- [x] **A curved line ignored `blend`.** Both routes that do not go through the segment shader, the GPU curve spans and the tessellated path a gap forces, built their pipeline with no mode at all, so a `basis` line under `multiply` composited normal. The span bind group was cached by curve kind alone as well, which is the same bug that lost a frame on the symbol mark. The new `blend-curves` fixture reads 0.003% of pixels and worst channel 53 where it read 5.530% and 161.

- [x] **Unreachable code passed both checks.** A `return` left above the rest of a function survived `npm run lint` and `npm run typecheck`, which is how a probe stayed in `needsBackdrop` through a green lint run. eslint's `no-unreachable` is off because the typescript preset defers to the compiler, and the compiler reports it as a suggestion rather than an error unless `allowUnreachableCode` is false. It is now, along with `allowUnusedLabels`.

- [x] **A fully transparent colour reached the frame as NaN.** d3 gives one NaN channels whatever was written, `rgba(0,0,0,0)` and `transparent` alike, and `parse` passed them through. A spec with a transparent background failed the frame outright, since a clear value has to be finite, and every mark with a transparent fill or stroke carried NaN into its vertex buffer where only the alpha discard hid it. The channels of a colour with no alpha are zero now.

- [x] **The symbol mark held a uniform bind group across a change of pipeline.** A bind group belongs to the layout it was made from, so a mark whose items carry different blends set one built for another pipeline and lost the whole command buffer. The same shape of bug as the line mark's, and it is keyed by pipeline now. Found by putting four blends on one symbol mark.

- [x] **A symbol shape with no distance function never blended.** `square` and the triangles have one and took a pipeline per mode. Everything else is triangulated and drew through the single shape pipeline, which carries none, so `cross`, `wedge` and a custom path composited normal whatever the item asked for. The run key already separated them by mode, which is what made it look right.

- [x] **A line past six and a half thousand points threw the whole frame.** The batch collected a mark's instances with `push(...values)`, and spreading throws `RangeError: Maximum call stack size exceeded` past about a hundred and twenty five thousand values. At nineteen floats an instance that is a line of roughly six and a half thousand points, which a dense time series reaches, and the throw took the frame rather than the mark. The batch holds each mark's array and copies them into one `Float32Array` at the flush instead, which also drops the intermediate JS array the flush used to build. `bigline.spec.ts` draws twelve thousand points and reads the frame back, and it fails with that exact error against the spread.

- [x] **Hostile input has a suite.** `test/render/scenes-hostile` feeds the renderer what a mark should never see: NaN and Infinity coordinates, zero and negative extents, values a billion pixels outside the viewport, absent styling, and shapes with too few points. Nothing is compared against canvas, since canvas has its own opinions about nonsense. What is asserted is that the device survives, the renderer keeps it, and the frame is not smeared: one NaN vertex stretches its triangle across everything, which is the failure the geographic miter bug produced. All five cases pass, drawing 0.4% and 0.8% of the frame where they draw at all.

  Two of the five failures it found first were the fixtures, not the renderer: JSON has no literal for NaN or Infinity, so those travel as sentinels the harness revives, and vega's own path parser throws on a malformed path string before any renderer sees it.

- [x] **Blank renders wherever there is no compositor.** `getCurrentTexture()` destroys the GPU device on a headless runner, roughly 15 ms after it is handed out, and every later `requestAdapter()` returns null. `configure()` and an offscreen pass both survive, and no launch flag combination changes it (seven were tested). `wgOptions.offscreen` renders into a texture the renderer owns and never touches the swapchain. Bisected by test/render/webgpu-smoke-diagnose.spec.ts.
- [x] **A destroyed device left the renderer dead.** A loss with reason `destroyed` returned early without clearing `_device`, so the renderer kept using it and every later capture rejected in `mapAsync`. Every loss reason now drops the device and its pipelines, capped at three automatic recoveries, and a failed capture retries once on a fresh device.
- [x] **Buffer uploads exhausted a constrained runner.** `mappedAtCreation` allocates a JS ArrayBuffer per buffer and a frame makes one per mark, so even a 32-byte uniform buffer failed to create. Uploads go through `queue.writeBuffer`, which reuses the staging ring.
- [x] **A frame's buffers were never freed.** A mark makes one per draw and WebGPU frees none on its own, so `jobs` was making 856 a frame and holding every one, climbing into tens of gigabytes while hovering. They are released once the frame after theirs has been submitted, late enough for a capture or a loaded image to have submitted again. A quad built once in `getResources` is marked lasting, since it outlives the frame that made it.
- [x] **Swapping renderers leaked a device.** vega swaps by dropping the old renderer and building a new one, never telling the old one to let go, so its device and everything on it stayed alive for as long as the page did. Taking over an element now finalizes whoever held it, and four swaps leave one live device rather than four.
- [x] **`renderLock` resolved `renderAsync` too early.** A render arriving mid-frame was coalesced into `_pendingRender`, but `_renderPromise` still pointed at the already-settled in-flight frame, so `renderAsync` (and the test harness) resolved before the newest scene was drawn, screenshotting a stale canvas. Deferred renders now expose a stand-in promise that settles only once the flushed frame does.
- [x] **A failed frame wedged the renderer.** `_render`'s `catch` cleared `_isRendering` but never flushed `_pendingRender`, so a queued render was silently dropped and its awaiter hung forever. All frame exits (completion, early return, failure) now drain through one `_finishFrame` path.
- [x] **The oversized-canvas early return stranded pending work** for the same reason. It now drains through `_finishFrame` too.
- [x] **Dead dirty-bounds tracking.** The `dirty()` override walked the group chain and unioned bounds into `_dirty`/`_tempb` on every item, but nothing ever read them, every frame redraws the whole scene. Removed, reinstate with partial redraw.
- [x] **Swapchain reconfigure.** `ctx.configure()` now runs once per device in `_reinit`, not per frame.

### Landed: tests

- [x] **CI draws its canvas reference on the CPU.** The runner has no GPU, and a desktop rasterizes canvas on its GPU, which differs in antialiased coverage and in how a dash wraps a closed corner. The cases that differ carry runner budgets, and `CPU_CANVAS=1` reproduces the runner's numbers locally. The presented-frame checks skip there, since the runner has no compositor.

- [x] **CI runs both pixel ratios.** Only dpr 1 ran there, though a finer grid moves every analytic edge onto different sample positions and eight fixtures carry a budget of their own for it, so a regression that showed only at dpr 2 was one somebody had to find by hand. The render job is a matrix over the two now, with the report and the images uploaded per ratio so they do not collide.

- [x] **A spec can be measured under a renderer option, not just at its default.** `canvasTextDrift` had one test of its own and the corpus only ever saw it off, so what it is worth was an argument rather than a number. A case is a spec plus a query now, and `label-drift` sits beside `label` in the gate and the gallery: 0.027% of pixels against 0.110%, with the worst channel and the signed mean unmoved at 226 and 2.91, which says the rest of that spec is the glyph fringe and not where the labels sit.

- [x] **A frame that breaks its own render pass is tested on the attachment shapes nothing else runs.** Every other spec renders multisampled and offscreen, so a single sampled frame and a presented canvas were both untouched by the mask and blend passes, and the backdrop copy reads whatever the frame resolved into, which for a presented canvas is the swapchain texture. `splitpass.spec.ts` draws three of those fixtures twice under each, watching `onuncapturederror` and checking ink came out, and it asserts the harness options actually reached the renderer rather than being ignored.

- [x] **A second measure, for how concentrated a difference is.** A whole-image percentage is diluted by however much of a chart matches, so a small region that is badly wrong reads like a faint haze. The densest 32px square is reported beside it with where to look, and across the corpus runs a median of 30x the whole-image figure and up to 633x. Gated at 35%, above the worst observed.
- [x] **A fidelity report ranks every case by how far it is from canvas.** `WEBGPU_FIDELITY=1 npx playwright test fidelity` renders all 118 specs and fixtures and ranks them by max and mean channel error, not just the share of pixels that moved. The two say different things: a soft antialiasing difference is a high pixel count with a low max, a misplaced or miscoloured mark is the reverse. This is what surfaced the image premultiplication bug, which the pixel count reported as 0.000%. 21 of 118 cases match canvas to within 2 levels everywhere.
- [x] **Five of the six specs that were 255 off were the glyph phase grid.** The earlier note said the phase quantization was not the cause. It was. At 8 steps per device pixel a label can land in a different one of skia's own subpixel buckets than canvas picked, and a whole stem pixel flips. 64 steps costs nothing measurable, since a static scene still has one phase per label and the glyph cache came out the same size (barley 37 entries either way, first frame 92.6 ms against 95.5, steady 10.9 against 10.3), and it only churns the cache faster while text is moving.
